Data

Excel or CSV, one row for each country. Codes (AU, AUS, 036), short names and formal names are all understood. Your file stays on this computer: nothing is uploaded.
Where to find data
Most of these let you choose countries and years and download a CSV or Excel file that opens here as it is.
  • World Bank Open Data – some 1,500 indicators for every country; DataBank to choose and download many at once
  • Our World in Data – every chart has a download with the full data as CSV
  • IMF World Economic Outlook – growth, inflation, debt and deficits, with forecasts
  • OECD Data Explorer – economy, tax, education and health, mainly for OECD members
  • UNdata – the UN system's statistics in one place
  • UN World Population Prospects – population, births, deaths and migration, with projections to 2100
  • WHO Global Health Observatory – health and disease
  • UNDP Human Development Reports – the Human Development Index and its parts
  • UNESCO Institute for Statistics – education and literacy
  • ILOSTAT – work, wages and unemployment
  • FAOSTAT – food, farming, land and forests
  • Ember – electricity generation and emissions
  • V-Dem – democracy and political regimes
  • Gapminder – long historical series, many back to 1800
Check each source's terms before you republish its data.

Columns

Colour each country by one column, shade it by another and hatch it by a third. Ctrl-click more columns for Colour to step through them. Dates in a column or in the headings (years, quarters, months) give a time line.
